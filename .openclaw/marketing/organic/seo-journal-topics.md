## Article 3 — "Why Garment-Dyed Cotton Ages Like Denim (And Reactive-Dyed Doesn't)"

**Target keyword:** `garment dyed cotton vs reactive dyed`

**Hook:** Two black tees. Same brand. After 30 washes one looks like a faded gym shirt and the other looks intentional. The difference is when the dye went on.

**Outline:**
- Reactive (or "package") dyed: fabric is dyed before cutting/sewing
- Garment-dyed: piece is constructed in undyed cotton, then dipped as a finished garment
- Why garment-dyed produces uneven, lived-in patina (the dye reaches different parts unequally)
- Why this is a feature for premium positioning (every piece slightly unique)
- Care instructions for garment-dyed pieces (cold wash, hang dry — extends the patina)
- The Vault Tee's Comfort-Colors-style process explained

## Article 4 — "Drop Shoulder vs Set-In Sleeve: The Construction Detail That Changes How a Hoodie Wears"

**Target keyword:** `drop shoulder vs set in sleeve hoodie`

**Hook:** A drop shoulder hoodie cut wrong looks slouchy and cheap. Cut right, it's the most flattering silhouette in menswear. Here's the difference.

**Outline:**
- Set-in sleeve: traditional construction, shoulder seam at the shoulder bone. Fits closer.
- Drop shoulder: shoulder seam falls 1-3" below the shoulder bone. Reads relaxed-luxury when proportions are right.
- The math: shoulder drop should equal ~5-8% of total body length, NOT a fixed "looser is better"
- Why fast-fashion drops are too long (compensating for wider chest measurements)
- BV's hoodie: 6.2% drop, GOTS organic 10.3oz cotton, double-stitched seams
- How to evaluate: lay the hoodie flat, measure shoulder seam to neck. If it's >4" you'll get a sloppy look.

## Article 5 — "Combed vs Carded Cotton: The Word in Every Premium Spec Sheet (and Why)"

**Target keyword:** `combed cotton vs carded cotton`

**Hook:** Both come from the same plant. Combed costs 30% more to produce. Here's exactly what changes between the two — and why every premium spec sheet specifies "combed ring-spun."

**Outline:**
- Cotton fiber length: longer fibers = stronger yarn, smoother finish, less pilling
- The carding step: separates fibers, produces medium-quality yarn (5% short fibers remain)
- The combing step (additional): removes the short fibers, leaving only the long ones
- Cost: combed is ~30% pricier to produce; takes more raw cotton per yard
- The feel difference: combed has visibly tighter weave, less halo, less pill after wash
- Ring-spun specifically: refers to the spinning method (vs open-end), produces more durable yarn
- Why "100% combed ring-spun cotton" is the spec sheet phrase to look for
- Side-by-side photo: combed ring-spun BV tee vs ring-spun carded fast-fashion equivalent after 20 washes

## Article 6 — "How to Tell Premium Apparel from Marketing (A Buyer's Field Guide)"

**Target keyword:** `how to tell premium quality clothing`

**Hook:** "Premium" is a marketing word, not a fabric. Here's how to evaluate apparel by feel + spec sheet, not by brand prestige.

**Outline:**
- The 5 tests:
  1. **GSM check** — does the brand publish weight? If not, walk away.
  2. **Fiber composition** — 100% something or a blend? Premium prefers 100% (cotton, merino, silk). Blends with synthetic = corner-cutting.
  3. **Stitch density** — count stitches per inch on a hem. <8 = cheap, 10-12 = mid, 12+ = premium.
  4. **Construction clue** — side-seamed vs tubular knit. Side-seamed costs 2× to make and lays flatter.
  5. **The fold test** — fold the piece and let go. Premium fabric holds its shape briefly. Cheap fabric collapses immediately.
- The brand language test: do they cite spec, mill, thread, construction? Or do they say "premium quality" + "luxury" + "elevated essentials" without numbers?
- Examples: BV's spec sheets vs a fast-fashion competitor's vague descriptions
- The price-quality relationship at premium tiers (you pay 2-3× for actual 5-10× durability — the math works long-term)

## SEO basics on each article

- **Slug** — short, includes primary keyword: `/blogs/journal/heavyweight-cotton-gsm-explained`
- **Title tag** — under 60 char with primary keyword + brand: "Heavyweight Cotton GSM Explained | Black Vault Apparel"
- **Meta description** — 140-155 char, summarizes value with primary keyword
- **H1** — exactly the article title
- **First paragraph** — primary keyword in first 100 words
- **Internal links** — at least 2 to product pages
- **External links** — 1-2 to authoritative sources (cotton industry reports, etc.)
- **Image alt text** — descriptive with secondary keywords
- **Schema markup** — Article schema (Shopify Horizon adds automatically if blog post type)
